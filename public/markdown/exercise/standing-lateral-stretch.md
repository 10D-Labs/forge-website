# Standing Lateral Stretch

## Quick answer

A standing side bend with one hand behind your head and that elbow pointing up. It stretches the lats and the side of your waist. Beginner back exercise using body weight. Targets lats, also works shoulders, obliques.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Lats
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Obliques
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Stretching

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and your knees slightly bent.
2. Raise one arm and put that hand behind your head, elbow pointing up. Let your other arm hang at your side.
3. Lean your upper body slowly away from the raised elbow until you feel a stretch along that side.
4. Hold for the set time, then come back up to standing straight and switch sides.

## Pro tips

- Reach your top arm overhead and actively pull toward the side you're stretching to for a deeper lat stretch.
- Keep your hips level and don't shift them to the opposite side, which reduces the stretch.
- Hold the stretched position for 20-30 seconds and breathe into the ribcage on the stretched side.

## Common mistakes

- Twisting the torso forward or backward instead of stretching directly to the side.
- Pushing the hips out to the opposite side instead of keeping them centered, which reduces the stretch on the lats.
- Holding for too short a duration to achieve meaningful tissue lengthening.

## FAQ

### What does the Standing Lateral Stretch target?

It primarily stretches the latissimus dorsi, obliques, intercostals (muscles between the ribs), and the quadratus lumborum (deep lower back muscle). It's one of the best stretches for side-body tightness.

### When should I do the Standing Lateral Stretch?

After upper body or back workouts when the lats and obliques are warm. It's also beneficial after long periods of sitting to relieve side-body stiffness.

### Is this stretch good for improving overhead mobility?

Yes. Tight lats restrict overhead arm movement. Regular lateral stretching can improve your overhead press range of motion and overhead squat position.

### What muscles does the Standing Lateral Stretch work?

The Standing Lateral Stretch primarily targets the lats, with secondary activation in the shoulders, obliques. It's classified as a stretching exercise for the back.

### Is the Standing Lateral Stretch good for beginners?

Yes, the Standing Lateral Stretch is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building lats strength.
