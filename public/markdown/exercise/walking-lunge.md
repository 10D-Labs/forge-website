# Walking Lunge

## Quick answer

Lunges that travel forward, each step taking you into the next lunge on the other leg. It works the glutes, with help from the quads and hamstrings. Intermediate legs exercise using body weight. Targets glutes, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Glutes
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and your hands on your hips.
2. Step forward with your right leg and lower your body until your back knee is just above the floor.
3. Keep your torso upright and your front knee over your ankle.
4. Push off your right foot and bring your left foot forward into the next lunge.
5. Keep alternating legs as you walk forward at a steady pace.

## Pro tips

- Take a long enough step that your front shin stays vertical at the bottom of the lunge; short steps push the knee too far forward.
- Drive up through the heel of your front foot to maximize glute activation as you step into the next rep.
- Keep your torso upright and avoid leaning forward, which shifts stress from the glutes to the lower back.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the front knee cave inward, which stresses the knee ligaments; actively push the knee out over the pinky toe.
- Taking short, choppy steps that turn the movement into a quad-dominant exercise and reduce glute and hamstring involvement.
- Rushing through the reps instead of pausing briefly at the bottom of each lunge to build control and balance.

## FAQ

### Are Walking Lunges better than stationary lunges?

Walking Lunges add a dynamic balance component and mimic real-world movement patterns. They also tend to recruit the glutes more because you're propelling yourself forward each rep.

### How many Walking Lunges should I do per set?

Aim for 10-15 steps per leg (20-30 total steps) per set. For muscle building, 3-4 sets with bodyweight or light dumbbells works well.

### Can Walking Lunges hurt your knees?

Not when done correctly. Keep your front knee tracking over your toes, take long strides, and avoid slamming your back knee into the ground. If you have existing knee issues, start with shorter range of motion.

### What muscles does the Walking Lunge work?

The Walking Lunge primarily targets the glutes, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Walking Lunge good for beginners?

The Walking Lunge is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of glutes strength with easier variations first.
