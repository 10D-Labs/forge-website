# Side Plank Hip Adduction

## Quick answer

A side plank with your top foot on a bench, lifting your bottom leg up to meet it. It works the inner thighs, with help from the obliques and glutes. Advanced legs exercise using body weight. Targets adductors, also works obliques, glutes.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Adductors
- **Secondary Muscles:** Obliques, Glutes
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Lie on your side next to a flat bench, propped on your forearm, elbow under your shoulder, top hand on your chest.
2. Rest your top foot on the bench with that leg straight, and let your bottom leg lie on the floor.
3. Lift your hips until your body is in a straight line, raising your bottom leg up to meet the bench.
4. Lower your hips and bottom leg slowly toward the floor. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Place your top foot on a bench or elevated surface and lift your bottom leg upward toward it to truly isolate the adductors.
- Keep your body in a perfectly straight line from head to feet; any sagging compromises adductor activation.
- Control the lowering phase of each rep for at least 2 seconds to maximize inner thigh engagement.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing this with hip abduction by lifting the top leg instead of the bottom leg, which completely changes the target muscle.
- Allowing the hips to drop or pike, reducing the stability demand and taking tension off the adductors.
- Placing the supporting elbow too far forward or behind the shoulder, which creates shoulder strain.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between Side Plank Hip Adduction and abduction?

Adduction involves lifting the bottom leg upward toward the midline, targeting the inner thigh. Abduction involves lifting the top leg away from the body, targeting the outer hip.

### Why is the Side Plank Hip Adduction considered advanced?

It requires simultaneously maintaining a side plank (core stability) while performing a controlled adductor lift, demanding strength in multiple muscle groups at once.

### How do I make the Side Plank Hip Adduction easier?

Start with a bent-knee side plank to reduce the core demand, or perform the adduction movement lying on your back without the plank component.

### What muscles does the Side Plank Hip Adduction work?

The Side Plank Hip Adduction primarily targets the adductors, with secondary activation in the obliques, glutes. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Side Plank Hip Adduction good for beginners?

The Side Plank Hip Adduction is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental legs movements first.
