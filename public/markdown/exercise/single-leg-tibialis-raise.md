# Single Leg Tibialis Raise

## Quick answer

A one-leg toe raise with your heel on a step, lifting the front of your foot toward your shin. It works the muscle on the front of the shin. Intermediate legs exercise using body weight. Targets calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Calves

- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand on one foot on a low step with only your heel on it and the front of your foot hanging off the edge.
2. Hook your other foot behind your ankle and rest one hand on a wall for balance.
3. Keeping your heel on the step, lift the front of your foot up toward your shin as far as you can.
4. Lower your toes slowly below the edge of the step. Do all your reps on one leg, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Stand on one foot with your heel on the edge of a step and the front of your foot hanging off.
- Lift the front of your foot toward your shin as high as you can, then lower it slowly.
- Hold a wall or rail for balance so you can focus on the shin muscle.

## Common mistakes

- Rushing the reps instead of feeling the front of the shin work.
- Only lifting the toes a small amount instead of using the full range.
- Rocking the body back to help lift the foot.

## FAQ

### What does the Single Leg Tibialis Raise work?

The tibialis anterior, the muscle on the front of your shin that lifts your foot. Most leg training ignores it.

### How often should I train the tibialis?

2-3 times per week with 2-3 sets of 15-25 reps per leg. It recovers quickly.

### Can this help with shin splints?

Strengthening the front of the shin is a common part of shin splint prevention, along with managing your running volume. See a professional if the pain persists.

### What muscles does the Single Leg Tibialis Raise work?

The Single Leg Tibialis Raise primarily targets the calves. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Single Leg Tibialis Raise good for beginners?

The Single Leg Tibialis Raise is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of calves strength with easier variations first.
