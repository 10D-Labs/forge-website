# Weighted Ring Pull Up

## Quick answer

A pullup on gymnastic rings with extra weight hanging from a belt at your waist. It works the lats and biceps. Advanced back exercise using rings. Targets lats, also works biceps, shoulders, core.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Rings
- **Target Muscle:** Lats
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Shoulders, Core
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Hang a weight plate from a dip belt around your waist.
2. Grab two gymnastic rings, palms facing away from you, and hang with your arms straight, knees bent and feet behind you.
3. Pull yourself up until your hands are level with your shoulders and your chin is above your hands.
4. Lower slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Hang the weight from a dip belt and start from a full hang with straight arms.
- Let the rings turn as you pull, finishing with your palms facing you and the rings near your chest.
- Lower yourself slowly back to a full hang.

## Common mistakes

- Adding weight before you can do strict bodyweight ring Pullups.
- Swinging or kipping, which makes the rings unstable.
- Dropping into the bottom instead of lowering with control.

## FAQ

### How are ring Pullups different from bar Pullups?

The rings move freely, so your wrists can turn into a comfortable position and your shoulders work harder to keep you steady.

### How much weight should I add?

Start with 5-10 lbs and add small amounts once you can do 3 clean sets of 5-8 reps.

### Should I use a weight vest or a dip belt?

Either works. A dip belt makes loading easy; a vest keeps the weight from swinging.

### What muscles does the Weighted Ring Pull Up work?

The Weighted Ring Pull Up primarily targets the lats, with secondary activation in the biceps, shoulders, core. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Weighted Ring Pull Up good for beginners?

The Weighted Ring Pull Up is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental back movements first.
