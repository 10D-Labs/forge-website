# Resistance Band Seated Hip Abduction

## Quick answer

A seated move where you push your knees apart against a band looped just above them. It works the outer hips, with help from the glutes. Beginner legs exercise using resistance band. Targets abductors, also works glutes, hamstrings.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Resistance Band
- **Target Muscle:** Abductors
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Hamstrings
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on a bench or chair with your back straight and your feet flat on the floor.
2. Loop a resistance band around your thighs, just above your knees, and hold the sides of the seat for support.
3. Push your knees apart slowly against the band.
4. Bring your knees slowly back together.

## Pro tips

- Sit tall with your back straight and push your knees apart against the band, not forward.
- Hold the open position for 1-2 seconds to maximize outer glute and abductor activation.
- Control the return and do not let the band snap your knees back together.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning back or forward during the movement, which changes the hip angle and reduces abductor isolation.
- Using a band that is too light, providing insufficient resistance for a training effect.
- Rushing through reps and letting the band control the eccentric (return) phase.

## FAQ

### What does the banded hip abduction target?

It targets the hip abductors, primarily the gluteus medius and minimus, which are responsible for pushing the knees apart and stabilizing the pelvis.

### Is the resistance band hip abduction good for glutes?

Yes, it specifically activates the gluteus medius, which is often underdeveloped. This is an excellent activation drill before squats or Deadlifts.

### What band strength should I use for Seated Hip Abductions?

Start with a light to medium band. You should feel significant resistance in the outer glutes but still be able to complete 15-20 controlled reps.

### What muscles does the Resistance Band Seated Hip Abduction work?

The Resistance Band Seated Hip Abduction primarily targets the abductors, with secondary activation in the glutes, hamstrings. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Resistance Band Seated Hip Abduction good for beginners?

Yes, the Resistance Band Seated Hip Abduction is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building abductors strength.
