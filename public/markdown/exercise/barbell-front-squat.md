# Barbell Front Squat

## Quick answer

A squat with the barbell resting across the front of your shoulders instead of your back. It works the glutes, quads and hamstrings. Intermediate legs exercise using barbell. Targets glutes, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, core.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Barbell
- **Target Muscle:** Glutes
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves, Core
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and your toes turned slightly out.
2. Hold the barbell across the front of your shoulders, against your collarbone, with your elbows raised in front so your upper arms are level with the floor.
3. Push your hips back and bend your knees to squat down, keeping your chest up.
4. Go down until your thighs are level with the floor, or as low as you comfortably can.
5. Push through your heels to stand back up.

## Pro tips

- Keep two or three fingers under the bar with elbows pushed high; you don't need a full grip as long as the bar sits on your front delts.
- Brace your core as if someone is about to punch you in the stomach before each rep to maintain torso rigidity.
- Focus on sitting straight down between your hips rather than pushing your hips back like a back squat.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the elbows drop during the ascent, causing the bar to roll forward and the torso to collapse.
- Rising hips-first out of the bottom, which turns the front squat into a good morning with the bar on your shoulders.
- Not going deep enough because the upright position is uncomfortable; work on ankle and thoracic mobility.

## FAQ

### Should I use a clean grip or cross-arm grip for front squats?

Clean grip is preferred for weightlifting transfer and forces better upper back position. Cross-arm is easier if you lack wrist mobility. Either works for building leg strength.

### Why are front squats so hard on my core?

The front-loaded barbell position demands intense core bracing to prevent forward collapse. If your core gives out before your legs, add front plank holds and heavy ab work to your routine.

### Can front squats replace back squats?

They can, especially if you have lower back issues. Front squats keep the torso more upright and place less stress on the spine. You won't be able to go as heavy, but they build excellent quad and core strength.

### What muscles does the Barbell Front Squat work?

The Barbell Front Squat primarily targets the glutes, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, core. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Barbell Front Squat good for beginners?

The Barbell Front Squat is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of glutes strength with easier variations first.
