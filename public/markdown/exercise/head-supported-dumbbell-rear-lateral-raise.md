# Head Supported Dumbbell Rear Lateral Raise

## Quick answer

A bent-over rear raise with your forehead resting on an incline bench, which keeps your upper body still. It works the backs of the shoulders, with help from the upper back. Intermediate shoulders exercise using dumbbell. Targets shoulders, also works traps, rhomboids.

- **Body Part:** Shoulders
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Shoulders
- **Secondary Muscles:** Traps, Rhomboids
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Set a bench to an incline and stand facing its top end, feet shoulder-width apart, with a dumbbell in each hand.
2. Bend your knees slightly, bend forward at the hips, and rest your forehead on the top of the bench, arms hanging down.
3. Raise your arms out to the sides, elbows slightly bent, until they're level with the floor.
4. Lower slowly back to the start.

## Pro tips

- Rest your forehead against a bench or incline pad to stabilize your torso and eliminate any body swing during the raise.
- Raise the dumbbells out to the sides with slightly bent elbows until arms reach parallel, squeezing the rear delts at the top.
- The head support lets you focus entirely on the rear delt contraction without worrying about lower back fatigue.

## Common mistakes

- Lifting the head off the support to generate momentum, which defeats the stability purpose.
- Pulling the dumbbells back toward the hips instead of raising them laterally, changing the exercise to a row pattern.
- Using too much weight and relying on the traps to shrug the dumbbells up instead of using the rear delts.

## FAQ

### Why support the head during rear Lateral Raises?

Head support stabilizes the torso, removes lower back strain from the bent-over position, and eliminates the temptation to use body momentum.

### What should I rest my head on?

The top edge of an incline bench set at a moderate height works well. A padded surface is more comfortable for longer sets.

### Is this better than unsupported rear Lateral Raises?

For isolation, yes. The support enforces stricter form. For functional training, unsupported versions challenge more stabilizer muscles.

### What muscles does the Head Supported Dumbbell Rear Lateral Raise work?

The Head Supported Dumbbell Rear Lateral Raise primarily targets the shoulders, with secondary activation in the traps, rhomboids. It's classified as a strength exercise for the shoulders.

### Is the Head Supported Dumbbell Rear Lateral Raise good for beginners?

The Head Supported Dumbbell Rear Lateral Raise is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of shoulders strength with easier variations first.
