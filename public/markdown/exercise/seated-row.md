# Seated Row

## Quick answer

A seated row on a machine with a chest pad, pulling the handles back toward you. It works the upper back. Beginner back exercise using machine. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Machine
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust the seat height and footrests. Sit with your chest against the pad and your feet on the footrests.
2. Hold the handles with your palms facing down, hands shoulder-width apart.
3. Keep your back straight and pull the handles toward your body, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
4. Let the handles go forward slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Sit tall with your chest against the pad and think about driving your elbows straight back behind you.
- Hold the peak contraction for a full second to ensure your rhomboids and mid-traps are fully engaged.
- Keep your shoulders depressed (pulled down away from your ears) throughout the entire movement.

## Common mistakes

- Rounding the upper back and letting the shoulders roll forward at the extended position.
- Using the lower back to jerk the weight by leaning backward excessively during the pull.
- Letting the weight stack touch down between reps, which releases all tension from the back muscles.

## FAQ

### What grip should I use on the Seated Row?

An overhand grip emphasizes the upper back and rear delts. A neutral grip balances lat and upper back work. An underhand grip shifts focus to the lats and biceps.

### How is the machine Seated Row different from the Cable Seated Row?

The machine has a fixed path of motion and chest pad for support, making it easier to isolate the back. The cable version requires more core stabilization and allows a freer range of motion.

### Is the Seated Row good for posture?

Yes. It strengthens the rhomboids, mid-traps, and rear delts, all of which are typically weak in people with forward-rounded shoulder posture.

### What muscles does the Seated Row work?

The Seated Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Seated Row good for beginners?

Yes, the Seated Row is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building upper back strength.
