# Barbell Side Split Squat

## Quick answer

A wide-stance squat with a barbell on your back, shifting your weight from one bent leg to the other while the opposite leg stays straight. It works the quads and glutes. Intermediate legs exercise using barbell. Targets quads, also works glutes, hamstrings, calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Barbell
- **Target Muscle:** Quads
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Hamstrings, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Rest a barbell across your upper back, hands wider than your shoulders, palms facing forward. Set your feet about twice shoulder-width apart, toes out a little.
2. Bend one knee and shift your hips toward that side, keeping the other leg straight, until the bent thigh is nearly level with the floor.
3. Stay low and shift across to the other side, straightening the first leg as you bend the other knee.
4. Keep alternating sides without standing up in between, with the bent knee pointing over your toes.

## Pro tips

- Use a wide stance (1.5-2 times shoulder width) with toes pointed out at 30-45 degrees.
- Shift your weight to one leg as you descend, bending that knee while keeping the other leg straighter.
- Push your hips back and keep your chest up as you lower into the split position.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the working knee collapse inward instead of tracking over the toes.
- Not going deep enough; aim to get the working thigh at least parallel to the ground.
- Rounding the upper back because the wide stance feels unnatural with a barbell.

## FAQ

### What's the difference between a side split squat and a lateral lunge?

They're very similar. A side split squat typically starts in the wide stance and shifts weight side to side, while a lateral lunge involves stepping out to the side from a standing position. The training effect is nearly identical.

### What muscles does the side split squat emphasize?

The quads and adductors (inner thigh) of the working leg get the most stimulus, along with the glutes. The wide stance and lateral component hit the adductors more than a standard squat.

### Is this exercise good for hip mobility?

Yes. The wide stance and deep descent stretch the adductors and hip flexors. Over time, this improves hip mobility and reduces the risk of groin strains.

### What muscles does the Barbell Side Split Squat work?

The Barbell Side Split Squat primarily targets the quads, with secondary activation in the glutes, hamstrings, calves. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Barbell Side Split Squat good for beginners?

The Barbell Side Split Squat is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of quads strength with easier variations first.
