# Dumbbell Seated Single Arm Rotate

## Quick answer

A seated forearm twist: lean forward with one arm hanging and turn the dumbbell a quarter turn and back. It works the forearms, with some help from the biceps. Beginner arms exercise using dumbbell. Targets forearms, also works shoulders, biceps.

- **Body Part:** Arms
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Forearms
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Biceps
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on the end of a flat bench with your feet flat and lean forward. Rest one hand on your thigh.
2. Hold a dumbbell in your other hand, that arm hanging straight down outside your knee, palm facing behind you.
3. Keeping your arm still, turn your forearm so your thumb swings forward and your palm ends up facing your knee.
4. Turn it back slowly to the start. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Keep your elbow fixed at your side and rotate the forearm outward slowly, focusing on the rotational muscles of the forearm.
- Use a very light dumbbell (3-8 lbs) since this targets the small pronator and supinator muscles.
- Perform the rotation through the full available range, going from internal to external rotation smoothly.

## Common mistakes

- Moving the entire upper arm instead of isolating the rotation to the forearm.
- Using too much weight, which forces the shoulder to assist and removes the forearm isolation.
- Performing the rotation too quickly, reducing the time under tension on the small rotational muscles.

## FAQ

### What muscles does the seated single arm rotate work?

The forearm pronators and supinators, along with the wrist stabilizer muscles. These small muscles are important for grip function and elbow health.

### Who should do forearm rotation exercises?

Anyone with forearm tightness, elbow pain, or who needs rotational forearm strength for sports like tennis, climbing, or martial arts.

### How often should I train forearm rotation?

2-3 times per week is sufficient. These are small muscles that recover quickly but also fatigue fast, so keep sets at 15-20 reps.

### What muscles does the Dumbbell Seated Single Arm Rotate work?

The Dumbbell Seated Single Arm Rotate primarily targets the forearms, with secondary activation in the shoulders, biceps. It's classified as a strength exercise for the arms.

### Is the Dumbbell Seated Single Arm Rotate good for beginners?

Yes, the Dumbbell Seated Single Arm Rotate is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building forearms strength.
