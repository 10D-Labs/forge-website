# Back Extension

## Quick answer

A seated machine exercise where you lean forward, then push your upper body back against a pad. It works the lower back, plus the glutes and hamstrings. Beginner back exercise using machine. Targets lower back, also works glutes, hamstrings.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Machine
- **Target Muscle:** Lower Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Hamstrings
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust the machine to fit your body. Sit with your back against the pad, secure your feet and hold the handles.
2. Lean forward slowly, letting your back round slightly, until you feel a stretch in your lower back.
3. Push back against the pad with your back muscles until you're sitting upright again.

## Pro tips

- Adjust the machine so the pivot point aligns with your hip crease, allowing your torso to hinge freely.
- Move through the range of motion slowly and deliberately, pausing at the top when your body forms a straight line.
- Hold a weight plate against your chest for added resistance once bodyweight becomes comfortable for 15+ reps.

## Common mistakes

- Hyperextending past neutral (going too far back), which compresses the lumbar spine.
- Rounding the back on the way down instead of hinging at the hips with a neutral spine.
- Using momentum to swing up quickly, which defeats the purpose of strengthening the spinal erectors.

## FAQ

### Are machine Back Extensions safe for people with lower back issues?

They can be beneficial for strengthening the lower back when done with controlled form and bodyweight only. However, start with a limited range of motion and consult a professional if you have disc issues.

### How often should I do Back Extensions?

2-3 times per week is ideal. They work well as a warm-up for heavy Deadlift days or as a finishing accessory exercise.

### What muscles does the Back Extension work?

The Back Extension primarily targets the lower back, with secondary activation in the glutes, hamstrings. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Back Extension good for beginners?

Yes, the Back Extension is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building lower back strength.
