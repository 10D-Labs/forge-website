# Kettlebell Single Arm Row

## Quick answer

A one-arm kettlebell row from a split stance, with your free hand braced on your knee. It works the upper back, with help from the biceps and forearms. Intermediate back exercise using kettlebell. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Kettlebell
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with one foot in front of the other, holding a kettlebell in one hand.
2. Bend your knees slightly and lean forward from your hips with your back straight, resting your free hand on your front knee.
3. Let the kettlebell hang with your palm facing down, then row it up toward your hip, keeping your elbow close to your body.
4. Lower it slowly until your arm is straight. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Hinge forward at about 45 degrees and brace your free hand on your knee or a bench for support.
- Row the kettlebell toward your hip rather than your shoulder, pulling with your elbow driving straight back.
- Squeeze your shoulder blade toward your spine at the top and hold for one second before lowering.

## Common mistakes

- Rotating the torso to heave the weight up instead of rowing with a stable trunk position.
- Shrugging the shoulder up toward the ear rather than keeping it depressed and retracted.
- Using a weight that's too heavy, causing the arm to do all the work while the back muscles barely engage.

## FAQ

### How is a kettlebell row different from a dumbbell row?

The kettlebell's center of mass sits below the handle, which slightly changes the resistance curve and grip demand compared to a dumbbell. The offset load can increase forearm and grip activation.

### Should I do rows one arm at a time or both together?

Single-arm rows allow you to use a free hand for support, which reduces lower back strain. They also let you identify and fix strength imbalances between sides.

### What muscles does the Kettlebell Single Arm Row work?

The Kettlebell Single Arm Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Kettlebell Single Arm Row good for beginners?

The Kettlebell Single Arm Row is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of upper back strength with easier variations first.
