# Band Push Situp

## Quick answer

A full situp against a band anchored behind you, pushing your arms forward as you rise. It works the abs, while the shoulders and chest steady your arms. Intermediate core exercise using resistance band. Targets abs, also works shoulders, chest.

- **Body Part:** Core
- **Equipment:** Resistance Band
- **Target Muscle:** Abs
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Chest
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Anchor the band behind your head. Lie on your back with your legs straight.
2. Hold the band with both hands, arms straight up above your chest.
3. Tighten your abs and sit all the way up, pushing your hands forward until your arms point straight ahead.
4. Lower yourself slowly until your back is on the floor and your arms point up again.

## Pro tips

- Press the band toward the ceiling as you sit up, fully extending your arms at the top of the movement.
- Keep the band tension constant by not letting it go slack at any point.
- Lower your torso back down slowly for a 2-3 second eccentric to maximize ab engagement.

## Common mistakes

- Pressing the band before completing the situp, which decouples the ab work from the pressing work.
- Using the arms to pull yourself up via the band rather than sitting up with your abs first.
- Anchoring the feet, which allows the hip flexors to dominate over the abs.

## FAQ

### What makes the push situp different from a regular situp?

The pressing component at the top adds shoulder and chest work, and the band tension requires more core control throughout the movement.

### How should the band be anchored?

Anchor the band at ground level behind your head. Hold the handles and sit up while pressing the band toward the ceiling, creating resistance against both the situp and press.

### Is this exercise suitable for beginners?

It is intermediate level. Master bodyweight situps and band presses separately before combining them.

### What muscles does the Band Push Situp work?

The Band Push Situp primarily targets the abs, with secondary activation in the shoulders, chest. It's classified as a strength exercise for the core.

### Is the Band Push Situp good for beginners?

The Band Push Situp is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of abs strength with easier variations first.
