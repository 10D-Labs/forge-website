# Posterior Tibialis Stretch

## Quick answer

A seated stretch where you loop a band around your foot and pull your toes back. It stretches the calf, including a deep muscle behind the shin. Beginner legs exercise using resistance band. Targets calves, also works hamstrings, quadriceps.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Resistance Band
- **Target Muscle:** Calves
- **Secondary Muscles:** Hamstrings, Quadriceps
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Stretching

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on the floor with one knee bent and that foot on the floor.
2. Loop the band around the ball of one foot and hold a handle in each hand.
3. Straighten that leg and gently pull the band toward you, drawing your toes back until you feel a stretch in your calf.
4. Hold for the set time, then relax and switch legs.

## Pro tips

- Pull the rope or band to evert your foot (sole turning outward) to specifically target the posterior tibialis on the inner calf.
- Maintain a straight knee and hold the position steadily for 20-30 seconds per leg.
- Apply gentle progressive tension rather than forcing the foot into an extreme position.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing eversion (targeting posterior tibialis) with dorsiflexion (targeting gastrocnemius/soleus), resulting in the wrong stretch.
- Pulling too hard and overstretching the medial ankle structures, which can cause pain.
- Not holding the stretch long enough for the deep muscle fibers to release.

## FAQ

### What does the posterior tibialis muscle do?

It supports the arch of the foot, inverts the ankle, and assists with plantarflexion. Dysfunction in this muscle is a leading cause of flat feet (fallen arches) in adults.

### Who should stretch the posterior tibialis?

Anyone with flat feet, shin splints (medial tibial stress syndrome), or inner ankle tightness. It is also important for runners who pronate excessively.

### Can I stretch this muscle without a band?

Yes. Sit with your leg crossed over the opposite knee and gently press the sole of your foot outward with your hand. A band just makes it easier to maintain the position.

### What muscles does the Posterior Tibialis Stretch work?

The Posterior Tibialis Stretch primarily targets the calves, with secondary activation in the hamstrings, quadriceps. It's classified as a stretching exercise for the legs.

### Is the Posterior Tibialis Stretch good for beginners?

Yes, the Posterior Tibialis Stretch is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building calves strength.
