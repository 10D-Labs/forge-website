# Horizontal Single Leg Press

## Quick answer

A leg press done one leg at a time on a seated, horizontal machine. It works the glutes, with help from the quads, hamstrings and calves. Intermediate legs exercise using machine. Targets glutes, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Machine
- **Target Muscle:** Glutes
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust the seat so your knee bends to about 90 degrees when your foot is on the footplate.
2. Sit with your back against the backrest and hold the handles at your sides.
3. Put one foot on the footplate and keep the other foot off it.
4. Press through your foot to straighten your leg, keeping your back against the backrest.
5. Bend your knee slowly to return to the start. Do all your reps with one leg, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Press through the center of your foot, driving evenly through the heel and ball of the foot.
- Control the eccentric by lowering the weight for 2-3 seconds to increase time under tension on the working leg.
- Avoid locking out the knee at the top to keep constant tension on the muscles.

## Common mistakes

- Allowing the knee to cave inward during the press, which indicates weak glute medius and can lead to knee injury.
- Pressing with the toes, which puts excessive force on the knee joint and reduces quad and glute activation.
- Using the same weight as bilateral Leg Press, forgetting that each leg now handles the full load independently.

## FAQ

### Why do Single Leg Press instead of regular Leg Press?

Single leg pressing addresses strength imbalances between legs, improves unilateral stability, and can help correct compensatory movement patterns.

### What weight should I use for Single Leg Press?

Start with about 40-50% of your bilateral Leg Press weight. The stabilization demand on one leg means you cannot simply halve your two-leg weight.

### Is the horizontal Leg Press easier on the back than a squat?

Yes, the seated position supports the spine and removes the axial loading, making it a safer option for people with lower back issues.

### What muscles does the Horizontal Single Leg Press work?

The Horizontal Single Leg Press primarily targets the glutes, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Horizontal Single Leg Press good for beginners?

The Horizontal Single Leg Press is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of glutes strength with easier variations first.
