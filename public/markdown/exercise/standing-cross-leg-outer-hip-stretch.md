# Standing Cross Leg Outer Hip Stretch

## Quick answer

A standing stretch with one leg crossed behind the other, pushing that hip out to the side. It stretches the outer hip and glutes. Beginner legs exercise using body weight. Targets abductors, also works glutes, hamstrings.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abductors
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Hamstrings
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Stretching

---

## How to perform

1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width apart and your arms at your sides.
2. Cross one leg behind the other and set that foot down on its toes, just outside your front foot.
3. Push your back leg's hip out to the side and lean your upper body the other way until the outside of that hip stretches.
4. Uncross your legs and stand tall. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Stand next to a wall for support and cross one leg behind the other.
- Push the hip of the back leg out to the side until you feel a stretch along the outer hip.
- Hold for 20-30 seconds, breathing slowly, then switch sides.

## Common mistakes

- Bending forward at the waist instead of pushing the hip out to the side.
- Bouncing into the stretch instead of holding it.
- Locking the knee of the back leg.

## FAQ

### What does this stretch target?

The outer hip and glutes, including the band of tissue along the outside of the thigh.

### When should I do it?

After workouts or runs, or whenever your outer hips feel tight. Hold 20-30 seconds per side, 2-3 times.

### Why use a wall?

Holding a wall lets you lean further into the stretch without losing your balance.

### What muscles does the Standing Cross Leg Outer Hip Stretch work?

The Standing Cross Leg Outer Hip Stretch primarily targets the abductors, with secondary activation in the glutes, hamstrings. It's classified as a stretching exercise for the legs.

### Is the Standing Cross Leg Outer Hip Stretch good for beginners?

Yes, the Standing Cross Leg Outer Hip Stretch is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building abductors strength.
