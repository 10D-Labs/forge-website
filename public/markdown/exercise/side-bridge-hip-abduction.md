# Side Bridge Hip Abduction

## Quick answer

A side plank on your forearm with a top-leg raise. It works the outer hips and glutes, with the obliques helping hold you up. Intermediate legs exercise using body weight. Targets abductors, also works glutes, obliques.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abductors
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Obliques
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Lie on your side with your legs straight and stacked on top of each other.
2. Prop yourself up on your forearm, with your elbow right under your shoulder.
3. Lift your hips off the floor until your body is in a straight line from head to feet.
4. Raise your top leg as high as you can without letting your hips turn.
5. Lower the leg back down. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Stack your hips vertically and avoid letting the top hip roll forward or backward during the leg lift.
- Squeeze your bottom-side obliques hard to keep your hips elevated while you lift the top leg.
- Lift your top leg only as high as you can without your hip dropping or rotating; quality beats height.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the hips sag toward the floor, which puts stress on the lower back instead of working the abductors and obliques.
- Rotating the top leg outward during the lift, which recruits the hip flexors instead of isolating the abductors.
- Holding breath throughout the hold, which increases intra-abdominal pressure unnecessarily and limits endurance.

## FAQ

### What does the Side Bridge Hip Abduction target?

It targets the hip abductors (outer thigh and glute medius) while also challenging the obliques and glutes to stabilize the side plank position.

### How long should I hold the side bridge position?

Aim for sets of 8-12 leg lifts per side while maintaining the bridge. If you cannot hold the bridge for the full set, start with fewer reps and build up.

### Is the Side Bridge Hip Abduction good for knee pain?

Strengthening the hip abductors can help stabilize the knee and reduce lateral knee pain, making this exercise a common recommendation in knee rehab programs.

### What muscles does the Side Bridge Hip Abduction work?

The Side Bridge Hip Abduction primarily targets the abductors, with secondary activation in the glutes, obliques. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Side Bridge Hip Abduction good for beginners?

The Side Bridge Hip Abduction is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of abductors strength with easier variations first.
