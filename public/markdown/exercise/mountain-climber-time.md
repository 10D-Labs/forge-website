# Mountain Climber (Time)

## Quick answer

A running motion done from the top of a pushup, driving your knees toward your chest one at a time. It raises your heart rate and works the abs, shoulders and triceps. Beginner cardio exercise using body weight. Targets cardiovascular, also works core, shoulders, triceps.

- **Body Part:** Cardio
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Cardiovascular
- **Secondary Muscles:** Core, Shoulders, Triceps
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Cardio

---

## How to perform

1. Start at the top of a pushup, hands under your shoulders and body in a straight line.
2. Bring your right knee toward your chest, then quickly switch and bring your left knee in.
3. Keep switching legs in a running motion at a steady pace, keeping your hips low.
4. Keep going for the set time.

## Pro tips

- Keep your hips at the same height as your shoulders; don't let them pike up or sag down.
- Drive each knee toward your chest with speed and intent, like sprinting horizontally.
- Stack your hands directly under your shoulders and spread your fingers wide for a stable base.

## Common mistakes

- Piking the hips up as you get tired, which takes tension off the core and turns it into a hip flexor exercise.
- Not bringing the knees far enough forward, reducing the range of motion and cardio benefit.
- Bouncing the toes off the ground instead of driving the knees with muscular force.

## FAQ

### How long should I do Mountain Climbers?

20-30 seconds at high intensity for power and conditioning, or 45-60 seconds at moderate pace for endurance. Rest for equal time between sets.

### Are Mountain Climbers good for abs?

Yes, they're one of the best dynamic core exercises. The plank position works the entire core isometrically while the knee drives work the hip flexors and lower abs.

### What's a good pace for Mountain Climbers?

For conditioning, aim for 40-60 knee drives per 30 seconds. For core-focused work, slow it down to a controlled 1-second-per-knee pace.

### What muscles does the Mountain Climber (Time) work?

The Mountain Climber (Time) primarily targets the cardiovascular, with secondary activation in the core, shoulders, triceps. It's classified as a cardio exercise for the cardio.

### Is the Mountain Climber (Time) good for beginners?

Yes, the Mountain Climber (Time) is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building cardiovascular strength.
