# Full Maltese

## Quick answer

A floor hold on straight arms with your hands out wide, body flat just above the floor and legs together, which makes it harder than the straddle version. It works the abs, with the shoulders and chest holding you up. Advanced core exercise using body weight. Targets abs, also works shoulders, chest.

- **Body Part:** Core
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abs
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Chest
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Lie face down and place your hands flat on the floor well wider than your shoulders, level with your waist.
2. Straighten your arms and lean forward to lift your whole body just off the floor.
3. Keep your legs straight and together, and your body flat and close to the floor.
4. Hold for a few seconds, then lower slowly back to the floor.

## Pro tips

- The Full Maltese requires years of dedicated ring training, so be patient with progressions and protect your joints.
- Maintain maximum body tension from head to toe, squeezing every muscle to create a rigid plank position.
- Train ring-specific straight-arm pressing strength with movements like maltese leans and ring planche work.

## Common mistakes

- Rushing the progression and attempting the Full Maltese before mastering the Straddle Maltese for 5+ seconds.
- Training this skill when fatigued, which massively increases the risk of a catastrophic shoulder injury.
- Neglecting balanced shoulder training, leading to overdeveloped anterior delts and weak posterior stabilizers.

## FAQ

### Is the Full Maltese the hardest ring skill?

It's among the most difficult. Along with the Victorian cross, it represents the pinnacle of straight-arm ring strength. Only elite gymnasts typically achieve it.

### Can I train toward a Full Maltese at home?

You need proper gymnastics rings at appropriate height. While home ring setups work for many skills, the maltese demands rings hung high enough to train safely with a spotter or crash mats.

### What's the difference between a maltese and a planche?

In a planche, your hands are beneath you (on the floor or parallettes). In a maltese, your arms are extended out to the sides on rings. The maltese is significantly harder due to the longer lever arm.

### What muscles does the Full Maltese work?

The Full Maltese primarily targets the abs, with secondary activation in the shoulders, chest. It's classified as a strength exercise for the core.

### Is the Full Maltese good for beginners?

The Full Maltese is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental core movements first.
