# Hanging Knee Raise

## Quick answer

A hanging core exercise where you lift your bent knees up to hip height. It works the abs and the muscles at the front of the hips. Advanced core exercise using body weight. Targets abs, also works hip flexors.

- **Body Part:** Core
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abs
- **Secondary Muscles:** Hip Flexors
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Hang from a pullup bar with your arms straight, hands about shoulder-width apart, palms facing away from you.
2. Without swinging, bend your knees and raise them in front of you until your thighs are level with the floor.
3. Lower your legs slowly until they hang straight again.

## Pro tips

- Hang from the bar with straight arms and lift your knees up to hip height with control.
- Tilt your pelvis up slightly at the top so your abs, not just your hip flexors, finish the rep.
- Pause for a second at the bottom of each rep to stop the swing before the next one.

## Common mistakes

- Swinging the body to throw the knees up instead of lifting them with the abs.
- Dropping the legs quickly instead of lowering them under control.
- Losing grip before the abs are tired. If your grip gives out first, use ab straps to hang from your elbows.

## FAQ

### Do Hanging Knee Raises work the lower abs?

Yes. They work the abs and the hip flexors. Curling your pelvis up at the top puts more of the work on the abs.

### How do I progress from Hanging Knee Raises?

Once you can do 3 sets of 15 controlled reps, lift your knees higher toward your chest, then start straightening your legs toward a Hanging Straight Leg Raise.

### What muscles does the Hanging Knee Raise work?

The Hanging Knee Raise primarily targets the abs, with secondary activation in the hip flexors. It's classified as a strength exercise for the core.

### Is the Hanging Knee Raise good for beginners?

The Hanging Knee Raise is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental core movements first.
