# Plank with Opposite Arm and Leg Raise

## Quick answer

A high plank where you lift one arm forward, then the opposite leg back, switching sides as you go. It works the abs, with the shoulders and glutes helping hold you steady. Intermediate core exercise using body weight. Targets abs, also works shoulders, triceps, glutes.

- **Body Part:** Core
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abs
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Triceps, Glutes
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Start in a high plank: hands on the floor under your shoulders, arms straight, body in a line from head to heels.
2. Lift one arm straight out in front of you to about head height, then put the hand back down.
3. Lift the opposite leg straight back to about hip height, then lower it. Keep your hips level.
4. Repeat with the other arm, then its opposite leg. Keep alternating sides.

## Pro tips

- Start in a high plank with your hands under your shoulders and your feet a little wider than hip width.
- Lift one arm forward, then the opposite leg back, and hold for a moment.
- Lower them and switch sides, keeping your hips level the whole time.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the hips rock or rotate when a hand or foot lifts.
- Lifting the leg too high, which arches the lower back.
- Rushing the switches instead of moving with control.

## FAQ

### What does this plank variation work?

The abs, with the shoulders and glutes helping to hold you steady on two points of contact.

### How do I make it easier?

Lift only the arm or only the leg at first, or do it from your knees.

### Why do my hips sway?

Your core is still building the strength to hold the position. Widen your feet and slow down until you can keep your torso still.

### What muscles does the Plank with Opposite Arm and Leg Raise work?

The Plank with Opposite Arm and Leg Raise primarily targets the abs, with secondary activation in the shoulders, triceps, glutes. It's classified as a strength exercise for the core.

### Is the Plank with Opposite Arm and Leg Raise good for beginners?

The Plank with Opposite Arm and Leg Raise is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of abs strength with easier variations first.
