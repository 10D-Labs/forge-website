# Hyperextension

## Quick answer

A back extension on a 45-degree bench: lower your upper body toward the floor, then raise it until it lines up with your legs. It works the lower back, plus the glutes and hamstrings. Beginner back exercise using body weight. Targets lower back, also works glutes, hamstrings.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Lower Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Hamstrings
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust a 45-degree hyperextension bench so your upper thighs rest on the pad, and hook your feet in place.
2. Cross your arms over your chest or put your hands behind your head.
3. Lower your upper body toward the floor, keeping your back straight.
4. Raise it back up until your body is in a straight line with your legs.

## Pro tips

- Position the pad at your upper thighs (not your hips) so you can hinge freely at the waist.
- Cross your arms on your chest or behind your head, and lower your torso until it's roughly perpendicular to your legs.
- Raise your torso until it's in line with your legs and no further to avoid lumbar Hyperextension.

## Common mistakes

- Lifting the torso too high past neutral, which compresses the lumbar discs and can cause lower back pain.
- Rounding the upper back during the lowering phase instead of maintaining a flat back.
- Using momentum to swing up quickly rather than engaging the erectors and glutes with a controlled tempo.

## FAQ

### Are Hyperextensions good for lower back pain?

When done correctly with bodyweight and controlled form, they can strengthen the spinal erectors and help prevent lower back pain. However, if you currently have acute back pain, consult a professional before performing them.

### Should I hold a weight for Hyperextensions?

Only once you can perform 15-20 bodyweight reps with perfect form and a 2-second pause at the top. Hold a plate against your chest or behind your head to add resistance.

### What muscles does the Hyperextension work?

The Hyperextension primarily targets the lower back, with secondary activation in the glutes, hamstrings. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Hyperextension good for beginners?

Yes, the Hyperextension is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building lower back strength.
