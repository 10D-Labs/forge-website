# Kettlebell Alternating Row

## Quick answer

A bent-over row with two kettlebells, pulling one up at a time and switching each rep. It works the upper back. Intermediate back exercise using kettlebell. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Kettlebell
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and knees slightly bent, holding a kettlebell in each hand, palms facing your body.
2. Bend forward at your hips, keeping your back straight.
3. Pull one kettlebell up toward your chest, keeping your elbow close to your body and drawing your shoulder blade back.
4. Lower it until your arm is straight, then row the other one. Keep alternating sides.

## Pro tips

- Hinge forward at the hips about 45 degrees with a flat back, letting the kettlebells hang directly below your shoulders.
- Row one kettlebell at a time while keeping the other arm extended. This challenges anti-rotation stability.
- Squeeze the shoulder blade back and hold for one second at the top of each row.

## Common mistakes

- Rounding the upper back during the hinge, which reduces upper back activation and risks lower back strain.
- Jerking the kettlebell up with body rotation rather than pulling with the back muscles.
- Standing too upright, which turns the row into a curl-like movement.

## FAQ

### How is the alternating row different from a standard Bent Over Row?

The alternating version adds an anti-rotation core demand because one side is loaded while the other is not. It also allows you to focus on each side independently to address strength imbalances.

### Should I use the same weight in both hands?

Yes, use equal weight in both hands to maintain balance. If one side is weaker, match the weight to the weaker side and do extra reps on that side rather than using unequal loads.

### What muscles does the Kettlebell Alternating Row work?

The Kettlebell Alternating Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Kettlebell Alternating Row good for beginners?

The Kettlebell Alternating Row is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of upper back strength with easier variations first.
