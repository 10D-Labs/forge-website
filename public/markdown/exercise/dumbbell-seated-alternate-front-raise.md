# Dumbbell Seated Alternate Front Raise

## Quick answer

A front raise done sitting on a bench, lifting one dumbbell at a time straight out in front of you to shoulder height. It works the front of the shoulders. Beginner shoulders exercise using dumbbell. Targets shoulders, also works trapezius, biceps.

- **Body Part:** Shoulders
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Shoulders
- **Secondary Muscles:** Trapezius, Biceps
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on a flat bench with your back straight and your feet flat on the floor.
2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand with your arms straight down at your sides, palms facing your body.
3. Keep your arm straight and raise one dumbbell in front of you until your arm is level with the floor.
4. Lower it slowly, then raise the other arm. Keep alternating sides.

## Pro tips

- Keep your back pressed against the bench and raise one dumbbell at a time to shoulder height with a straight arm and palm facing down.
- Alternate arms smoothly, lowering one as the other rises, to maintain a steady rhythm.
- Avoid swinging the body or pushing off the bench with your back; the seated position should enforce strict form.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning back to assist the raise, which uses momentum and reduces anterior deltoid isolation.
- Raising the dumbbell above shoulder height, which shifts the load from the front delt to the upper traps.
- Rushing the alternating pattern and not giving each arm a complete range of motion.

## FAQ

### Why do front raises seated instead of standing?

The seated position prevents leg drive and body sway, forcing stricter isolation of the anterior deltoid.

### Is alternating arms better than raising both at once?

Alternating allows you to focus on each shoulder individually and slightly extends the set duration, increasing time under tension per arm.

### Do front raises work the side delts at all?

Minimally. Front raises primarily target the anterior (front) deltoid. For side delt development, pair them with Lateral Raises.

### What muscles does the Dumbbell Seated Alternate Front Raise work?

The Dumbbell Seated Alternate Front Raise primarily targets the shoulders, with secondary activation in the trapezius, biceps. It's classified as a strength exercise for the shoulders.

### Is the Dumbbell Seated Alternate Front Raise good for beginners?

Yes, the Dumbbell Seated Alternate Front Raise is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building shoulders strength.
