# Frankenstein Squat

## Quick answer

A squat with the barbell balanced on the front of your shoulders and your arms held straight out, which makes you stay upright. It works the glutes, quads and hamstrings. Intermediate legs exercise using barbell. Targets glutes, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Barbell
- **Target Muscle:** Glutes
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and rest a barbell on the front of your shoulders.
2. Hold your arms straight out in front of you, palms down, with your hands off the bar.
3. Bend your knees and hips to sit back as if into a chair. Keep your back straight.
4. Lower until your thighs are level with the floor, or as low as you comfortably can.
5. Push through your heels to stand back up.

## Pro tips

- Hold the barbell in front of you with straight arms, parallel to the floor. This is a front-loaded squat that requires an upright torso.
- Keep your arms perfectly horizontal throughout the squat; if the bar dips, your core is disengaging.
- Descend slowly and maintain a vertical torso. Any forward lean will cause you to lose the bar.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the arms drop during the squat, which means the core is not braced properly and the bar becomes unstable.
- Leaning forward, which shifts the load and defeats the purpose of the Frankenstein position (which is to enforce an upright torso).
- Using too much weight; this exercise is about positioning and core engagement, not maximal loading.

## FAQ

### What is the purpose of the Frankenstein Squat?

It teaches an upright torso position for front squats by removing the rack position. If you lean forward at all, the bar rolls off your hands, providing instant feedback on your positioning.

### Is the Frankenstein Squat a front squat variation?

Yes, it is a front squat with the arms extended straight forward (like Frankenstein's monster), which makes it impossible to lean forward and teaches proper front squat posture.

### How much weight should I use for Frankenstein Squats?

Use very light weight (an empty bar or 50-65 lbs). This exercise is a technique drill, not a strength exercise. The goal is to perfect upright squat positioning.

### What muscles does the Frankenstein Squat work?

The Frankenstein Squat primarily targets the glutes, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Frankenstein Squat good for beginners?

The Frankenstein Squat is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of glutes strength with easier variations first.
