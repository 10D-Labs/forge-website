# Reverse Crunch

## Quick answer

A crunch done by curling your hips off the floor and bringing your knees toward your chest. It works the abs, especially the lower part. Beginner core exercise using body weight. Targets abs, also works hip flexors.

- **Body Part:** Core
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Abs
- **Secondary Muscles:** Hip Flexors
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Lie on your back with your arms flat along your sides.
2. Bend your knees and lift your feet off the floor until your thighs point straight up.
3. Tighten your abs and curl your hips off the floor, bringing your knees toward your chest.
4. Lower your hips slowly until your thighs point straight up again.

## Pro tips

- Focus on curling your pelvis up toward your ribcage rather than just lifting your knees toward your face.
- Keep your arms flat on the floor beside you for stability but avoid pressing down hard to cheat the rep.
- Lower your hips back down slowly for a 2-3 second count to maintain tension on the abs.

## Common mistakes

- Using momentum to swing the legs up instead of rolling the pelvis with controlled ab contraction.
- Lifting the hips straight up (like a leg raise) instead of curling them toward the ribcage in a crunch pattern.
- Letting the legs fall back to the starting position without controlling the eccentric.

## FAQ

### Do Reverse Crunches target lower abs?

Yes. The Reverse Crunch preferentially targets the lower portion of the rectus abdominis because the pelvis moves toward the ribcage rather than the other way around.

### Are Reverse Crunches safe for people with lower back pain?

Generally yes, because the lower back stays supported on the floor. They are often safer than situps for people with lumbar issues.

### How many Reverse Crunches should you do?

3 sets of 12-20 reps with controlled tempo. If you can do more than 20, slow down the eccentric or add a pause at the top.

### What muscles does the Reverse Crunch work?

The Reverse Crunch primarily targets the abs, with secondary activation in the hip flexors. It's classified as a strength exercise for the core.

### Is the Reverse Crunch good for beginners?

Yes, the Reverse Crunch is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building abs strength.
