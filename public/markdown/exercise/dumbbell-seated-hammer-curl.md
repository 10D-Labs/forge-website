# Dumbbell Seated Hammer Curl

## Quick answer

A two-arm curl done sitting against a back rest, with your palms facing each other, thumbs up. It works the biceps and the forearms. Beginner arms exercise using dumbbell. Targets biceps, also works forearms.

- **Body Part:** Arms
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Biceps
- **Secondary Muscles:** Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on a bench with a back rest, holding a dumbbell in each hand with your arms straight down and palms facing your body.
2. Keep your upper arms still and your palms facing in (thumbs up) as you curl both dumbbells up to shoulder level.
3. Lower the dumbbells slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Sit upright with your arms hanging straight down at your sides, palms facing your torso, before beginning the curl.
- Curl without rotating your wrists; the neutral grip should be maintained from start to finish.
- Squeeze hard at the top and control the descent for at least 2 seconds.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the dumbbells swing outward at the bottom rather than keeping them close to your sides.
- Leaning back to help curl the weight, especially as the set becomes difficult.
- Curling too fast and relying on momentum rather than controlled muscular contraction.

## FAQ

### Are seated hammer curls as effective as standing?

More effective for isolation since the seated position eliminates body swing. You'll use slightly less weight but get better brachialis and brachioradialis engagement.

### Can I do seated hammer curls on a bench without back support?

You can, but a bench with back support is preferable because it prevents you from leaning back and cheating.

### Should I curl both arms together or alternate?

Both approaches work. Curling together saves time. Alternating provides a brief rest between arms, allowing slightly more weight or reps per arm.

### What muscles does the Dumbbell Seated Hammer Curl work?

The Dumbbell Seated Hammer Curl primarily targets the biceps, with secondary activation in the forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the arms.

### Is the Dumbbell Seated Hammer Curl good for beginners?

Yes, the Dumbbell Seated Hammer Curl is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building biceps strength.
