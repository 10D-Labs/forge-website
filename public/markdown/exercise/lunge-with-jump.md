# Lunge with Jump

## Quick answer

A reverse lunge that ends in a jump, driving your back knee up to hip height as you leave the floor. It works the glutes and quads, with help from the hamstrings and calves. Advanced legs exercise using body weight. Targets glutes, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, calves.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Glutes
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Plyometrics

---

## How to perform

1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width apart and your elbows bent at your sides.
2. Step one foot back and lower until your back knee nearly touches the floor.
3. Push off your front foot and jump up, driving your back knee up to hip height.
4. Land softly on your front foot, set the other foot down beside it, and stand tall.
5. Step the other foot back for the next rep. Keep alternating sides.

## Pro tips

- Land softly on the balls of your feet and immediately sink into the lunge to absorb impact through your muscles, not joints.
- Swing your arms upward as you jump to generate extra height and allow time for the leg switch.
- Keep your front knee aligned over your ankle on every landing; don't let it shoot past your toes.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning the torso too far forward on the landing, which puts excessive stress on the front knee.
- Not switching legs fully in the air, landing with feet too close together and losing balance.
- Taking too shallow a stance on the landing, which forces the front knee past the toes.

## FAQ

### Are jump lunges bad for your knees?

They're a high-impact exercise that requires good landing mechanics. If you have knee issues, master regular lunges first and start with low, gentle jumps before going for height.

### How many jump lunges should I do?

8-12 per leg for power development, or 30-60 seconds for conditioning. Rest fully between power sets, or keep rest short for metabolic work.

### Can I do jump lunges every day?

The plyometric impact is demanding on joints and muscles. Limit jump lunges to 2-3 times per week with at least one rest day between sessions.

### What muscles does the Lunge with Jump work?

The Lunge with Jump primarily targets the glutes, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves. It's classified as a plyometrics exercise for the legs.

### Is the Lunge with Jump good for beginners?

The Lunge with Jump is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental legs movements first.
