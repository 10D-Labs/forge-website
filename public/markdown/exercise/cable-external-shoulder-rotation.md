# Cable External Shoulder Rotation

## Quick answer

A standing cable move that swings the forearm outward while the elbow stays pinned to your side. It works the rear shoulders and the small muscles that steady the joint. Beginner shoulders exercise using cable. Targets shoulders, also works rotator cuff, trapezius.

- **Body Part:** Shoulders
- **Equipment:** Cable
- **Target Muscle:** Shoulders
- **Secondary Muscles:** Rotator Cuff, Trapezius
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Rehabilitation

---

## How to perform

1. Set a cable pulley at elbow height, attach a single handle, and stand side-on to the machine.
2. Hold the handle in the hand farther from the machine, palm facing your stomach, thumb up.
3. Bend that elbow 90 degrees and tuck it against your side, forearm across your belly.
4. Keep your elbow at your side and swing your forearm out, away from the machine, until your hand points out to the side.
5. Let it come back slowly across your belly. Do all your reps with one arm, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Keep your elbow bent at 90 degrees and pinned to your side. Rotate your forearm outward, away from your body, using only the rotator cuff muscles.
- Use very light weight (2-5 lbs). This is a prehab exercise targeting the infraspinatus and teres minor, not a strength movement.
- Place a rolled towel between your elbow and torso to maintain proper arm position and prevent the elbow from drifting.

## Common mistakes

- Using too much weight, which causes the body to compensate by rotating the trunk or shrugging the shoulder.
- Letting the elbow pull away from the torso during the rotation, which changes the axis of rotation and reduces rotator cuff isolation.
- Performing the reps too quickly without controlling the return phase, which misses the eccentric strengthening benefit.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between internal and external shoulder rotation?

External rotation moves the forearm away from the body (targeting infraspinatus and teres minor), while internal rotation moves it toward the body (targeting subscapularis). Both are needed for balanced rotator cuff strength.

### When should I do external shoulder rotations?

Include them in your warm-up before pressing or overhead movements, or at the end of upper body workouts. They help prevent rotator cuff injuries and improve shoulder stability.

### What muscles does the Cable External Shoulder Rotation work?

The Cable External Shoulder Rotation primarily targets the shoulders, with secondary activation in the rotator cuff, trapezius. It's classified as a rehabilitation exercise for the shoulders.

### Is the Cable External Shoulder Rotation good for beginners?

Yes, the Cable External Shoulder Rotation is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building shoulders strength.
