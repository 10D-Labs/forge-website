# Chinup

## Quick answer

A pullup with your palms facing you and your hands shoulder-width apart, pulling until your chin clears the bar. It works the lats, plus the biceps and forearms. Intermediate back exercise using body weight. Targets lats, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Lats
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Hang from a pullup bar with your palms facing you and your hands shoulder-width apart.
2. Tighten your abs and pull yourself up, leading with your chest, until your chin is above the bar.
3. Lower yourself slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Initiate the pull by driving your elbows down and back rather than curling with your biceps first.
- Cross your ankles behind you and squeeze your glutes to prevent swinging and maintain a straight body line.
- Aim to get your chin clearly above the bar and hold for a brief pause before lowering with control.

## Common mistakes

- Using a kipping motion or swinging the legs to get the chin over the bar instead of pulling with the back and arms.
- Only performing half reps by not fully extending the arms at the bottom, which limits lat stretch.
- Craning the neck forward to get the chin over the bar rather than actually pulling the body higher.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a Chinup and a Pullup?

A Chinup uses a supinated (palms facing you) grip, which recruits more biceps. A Pullup uses a pronated (palms away) grip, which emphasizes the lats and forearms more.

### How many Chinups should I be able to do?

For general fitness, 8-12 unassisted Chinups is a solid benchmark. If you cannot do one yet, start with band-assisted or negative (eccentric-only) Chinups.

### Are Chinups better than lat pulldowns?

Chinups are generally superior for overall back development because they are a closed-chain movement that recruits more stabilizers. Pulldowns are useful for beginners or for high-rep isolation work.

### What muscles does the Chinup work?

The Chinup primarily targets the lats, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Chinup good for beginners?

The Chinup is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of lats strength with easier variations first.
