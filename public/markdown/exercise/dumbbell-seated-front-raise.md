# Dumbbell Seated Front Raise

## Quick answer

A front raise done sitting on a bench, which cuts down on swinging. It works the front of the shoulders. Beginner shoulders exercise using dumbbell. Targets shoulders, also works trapezius, biceps.

- **Body Part:** Shoulders
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Shoulders
- **Secondary Muscles:** Trapezius, Biceps
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Sit on a bench with your feet flat on the floor and a dumbbell in each hand, resting on your thighs.
2. Keep your back straight and raise the dumbbells in front of you, palms facing down, until they reach shoulder height.
3. Lower them slowly back to your thighs.

## Pro tips

- Raise both dumbbells in front of you to shoulder height with palms facing down, keeping your arms straight throughout.
- Sit tall with your back against the bench and resist the urge to lean back as the dumbbells get heavier at shoulder height.
- Lower the dumbbells slowly over 2 seconds to keep constant tension on the anterior delts.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning back against the bench and arching to assist the raise, which uses momentum instead of front delt strength.
- Raising the dumbbells above shoulder height, which recruits the traps and takes tension off the front delts.
- Using too much weight and swinging the arms up rather than raising them with controlled deltoid contraction.

## FAQ

### Do seated front raises hit the shoulders differently than standing?

They target the same anterior delt but the seated position eliminates body sway, making each rep stricter and more isolating.

### Should I do front raises with palms down or palms in?

Palms down (pronated) emphasizes the anterior delt more. Palms in (neutral) adds some brachioradialis involvement but is easier on the shoulders.

### Can front raises cause shoulder impingement?

They can if you raise above shoulder height or use excessive internal rotation. Stop at shoulder height and keep a neutral wrist position.

### What muscles does the Dumbbell Seated Front Raise work?

The Dumbbell Seated Front Raise primarily targets the shoulders, with secondary activation in the trapezius, biceps. It's classified as a strength exercise for the shoulders.

### Is the Dumbbell Seated Front Raise good for beginners?

Yes, the Dumbbell Seated Front Raise is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building shoulders strength.
