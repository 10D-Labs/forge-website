# High Knee Against Wall

## Quick answer

Running in place with high knees while leaning on a wall for support. It raises your heart rate and works the quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves. Beginner cardio exercise using body weight. Targets cardiovascular, also works quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, calves.

- **Body Part:** Cardio
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Cardiovascular
- **Secondary Muscles:** Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Cardio

---

## How to perform

1. Stand facing a wall with your feet hip-width apart and lean in with your hands on the wall.
2. Lift your right knee toward your chest, keeping your left foot on the floor.
3. Switch quickly, bringing your left knee up as your right foot comes down.
4. Keep alternating legs fast, like running in place, with your knees as high as you can and your upper body steady.

## Pro tips

- Drive each knee up explosively while pushing off the ball of the grounded foot to mimic sprinting mechanics.
- Keep a slight forward lean into the wall with your arms providing light support, not holding you up.
- Focus on fast, rhythmic leg exchanges rather than how high each knee gets.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning too heavily into the wall and supporting body weight through the arms instead of the legs.
- Letting the knees drift outward instead of driving them straight up toward the chest.
- Moving the upper body excessively instead of keeping it stable while the legs drive.

## FAQ

### Why do high knees against a wall instead of standing?

The wall provides a slight forward lean that mimics sprint position and helps you focus purely on knee drive mechanics without worrying about balance or forward movement.

### How long should each set be?

20-30 seconds at maximum effort for sprint training, or 45-60 seconds at moderate pace for cardio conditioning. Rest for equal or double the work time.

### Are wall high knees a good warm-up?

Yes, they elevate your heart rate, activate the hip flexors and glutes, and rehearse sprinting mechanics. They're a staple warm-up drill for runners and athletes.

### What muscles does the High Knee Against Wall work?

The High Knee Against Wall primarily targets the cardiovascular, with secondary activation in the quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, calves. It's classified as a cardio exercise for the cardio.

### Is the High Knee Against Wall good for beginners?

Yes, the High Knee Against Wall is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building cardiovascular strength.
