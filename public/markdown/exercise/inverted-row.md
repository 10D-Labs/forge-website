# Inverted Row

## Quick answer

A bodyweight row hanging back from a waist-high bar and pulling your chest up to it. It works the upper back, with help from the biceps and forearms. Beginner back exercise using body weight. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Set a bar in a rack at waist height. Stand facing it with your feet shoulder-width apart.
2. Grab the bar with your palms facing down, hands slightly wider than shoulder-width.
3. Walk your feet forward under the bar and lean back until your arms are straight, keeping your body straight and your heels on the floor.
4. Pull your chest up to the bar.
5. Lower yourself slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Set the bar at waist height and walk your feet forward until your body is at a challenging angle (roughly 45 degrees for intermediate).
- Pull with your elbows driving straight back, not out to the sides, and finish with the bar touching your lower chest.
- Squeeze your shoulder blades together for a full second at the top of each rep.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the hips sag or pike, breaking the straight-line body position that makes this exercise effective.
- Using a partial range of motion by not pulling the chest all the way to the bar.
- Looking up at the bar during the pull, which hyperextends the neck instead of keeping it neutral.

## FAQ

### What muscles do Inverted Rows work?

Primarily the rhomboids, mid/lower traps, rear deltoids, and lats, with significant bicep involvement. They also work the core isometrically to maintain a rigid body position.

### How do I make Inverted Rows easier or harder?

Easier: bend your knees or set the bar higher so you're more upright. Harder: elevate your feet, add a weighted vest, or use a pause at the top of each rep.

### What muscles does the Inverted Row work?

The Inverted Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Inverted Row good for beginners?

Yes, the Inverted Row is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building upper back strength.
