# Dumbbell Side Plank with Rear Fly

## Quick answer

A plank that turns into a side plank as you lift a dumbbell toward the ceiling with a straight arm. It works the upper back and shoulders while your core holds you steady. Advanced back exercise using dumbbell. Targets upper back, also works shoulders, core.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Core
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Start in a high plank on straight arms with your feet hip-width apart.
2. Hold a dumbbell in one hand just off the floor under your chest, palm facing your other hand.
3. Keep your hips up and roll into a side plank on your other arm, lifting the dumbbell with a straight arm toward the ceiling.
4. Lower the dumbbell slowly as you turn back into the plank. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Establish a rock-solid side plank before initiating any fly movement with the dumbbell.
- Keep the lifting arm straight with only a slight bend in the elbow as you raise the dumbbell toward the ceiling.
- Use a very light dumbbell (3-8 lbs) since the unstable side plank position makes even light weights very challenging.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the hips sag as you lift the dumbbell, which means your core can't handle the combination of movements.
- Using too heavy a dumbbell, causing the torso to rotate and collapse the side plank.
- Bending the arm excessively during the fly, turning it into a row instead of a rear fly.

## FAQ

### What makes this exercise effective?

It combines anti-lateral flexion core work (side plank) with upper back strengthening (rear fly) in one movement. It's extremely time-efficient and builds real-world functional stability.

### What weight should I use?

Start with 3-5 lbs, seriously. The side plank position is already demanding, and adding any rear fly movement makes it exponentially harder.

### Can I do this on my hand instead of forearm?

Yes, performing the side plank on your hand (straight arm) is harder and requires more shoulder stability. Start on the forearm and progress to the hand as you get stronger.

### What muscles does the Dumbbell Side Plank with Rear Fly work?

The Dumbbell Side Plank with Rear Fly primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the shoulders, core. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Dumbbell Side Plank with Rear Fly good for beginners?

The Dumbbell Side Plank with Rear Fly is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental back movements first.
