# Bodyweight Standing Single Arm Row

## Quick answer

A one-arm row using your body weight, leaning back from wall bars and pulling your chest in. It works the upper back, with help from the biceps and forearms. Beginner back exercise using body weight. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand facing wall bars with your toes near the base. Grab a rung at chest height with one hand, palm facing down.
2. Lean back until your arm is straight, keeping your body in a straight line from head to heels.
3. Pull your chest toward the bars, driving your elbow back past your side.
4. Lower yourself slowly until your arm is straight. Do all your reps on one side, then switch.

## Pro tips

- Grab a sturdy doorframe, post, or rack with one hand and lean back until your arm is fully extended.
- Pull yourself toward the anchor by driving your elbow back and squeezing your shoulder blade.
- Keep your body straight from head to heels and resist rotating as you pull.

## Common mistakes

- Not leaning back far enough, which reduces the bodyweight resistance and makes the exercise too easy.
- Rotating the torso toward the pulling arm instead of maintaining a square torso throughout the movement.
- Using the free arm to push against the anchor for assistance, which defeats the single-arm isolation purpose.

## FAQ

### What equipment do I need for bodyweight single arm rows?

Any stable vertical surface you can grip: a doorframe, a power rack upright, a sturdy pole, or even a tree trunk. No special equipment is needed.

### Are bodyweight rows effective for building back muscles?

Yes, especially for beginners and intermediate lifters. The single-arm version also challenges anti-rotation core stability, adding a functional training component.

### How do I make bodyweight rows harder?

Lean back further to increase the angle, elevate your feet, or slow down the tempo. You can also pause for 2-3 seconds at the top of each rep.

### What muscles does the Bodyweight Standing Single Arm Row work?

The Bodyweight Standing Single Arm Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Bodyweight Standing Single Arm Row good for beginners?

Yes, the Bodyweight Standing Single Arm Row is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building upper back strength.
