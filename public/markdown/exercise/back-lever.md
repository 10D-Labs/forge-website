# Back Lever

## Quick answer

An advanced hold on gymnastics rings: you hang face down with your body straight and level with the floor, arms straight behind you. It works the upper back, with the biceps, forearms and core helping. Advanced back exercise using body weight. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms, core.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms, Core
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Hang from a pair of gymnastics rings with your arms straight and palms facing each other.
2. Tuck your knees to your chest and roll backward so your hips pass up between your arms and you're upside down.
3. Straighten your body and lower it behind you until it's level with the floor, face down, arms straight. Keep your body in one straight line.
4. Hold for a few seconds, then tuck your knees and roll back the way you came to a hang.

## Pro tips

- Begin with a German hang to develop the shoulder extension flexibility this position demands.
- Tuck your knees first and slowly extend one leg at a time to find your balance point.
- Protract your shoulder blades (push them apart) to create a stable shelf for your body to rest on.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the Skin the Cat progression and jumping straight to the Back Lever without adequate shoulder mobility.
- Arching the lower back to compensate for weak core engagement, which puts strain on the lumbar spine.
- Holding the position with internally rotated shoulders, which increases impingement risk.

## FAQ

### Is the Back Lever safe for your shoulders?

It places significant demand on shoulder extension and the bicep tendons. Build up gradually through German hangs and tuck Back Lever progressions to prepare your connective tissue.

### What's the difference between a Front Lever and a Back Lever?

In a Front Lever you face up; in a Back Lever you face down. The Back Lever demands more shoulder extension flexibility, while the Front Lever demands more lat and core strength.

### How long should I hold a Back Lever?

A 10-second hold with good form is a solid benchmark. Build up in 2-3 second increments from tuck progressions.

### What muscles does the Back Lever work?

The Back Lever primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms, core. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Back Lever good for beginners?

The Back Lever is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental back movements first.
