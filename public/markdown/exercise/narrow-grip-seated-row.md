# Narrow Grip Seated Row

## Quick answer

A seated machine row with your chest against a pad, pulling narrow handles with your palms facing each other. It works the upper back, with help from the biceps and forearms. Beginner back exercise using machine. Targets upper back, also works biceps, forearms.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Machine
- **Target Muscle:** Upper Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Biceps, Forearms
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust the seat height and footrests. Sit with your feet flat on the footrests and your knees slightly bent.
2. Lean slightly forward with your chest against the pad and grab the narrow handles, palms facing each other.
3. Keep your back straight and pull both handles toward your body.
4. Let the handles go forward slowly until your arms are straight.

## Pro tips

- Sit tall with a slight lean forward at the start, then pull the handles to your lower ribcage while bringing your torso to vertical.
- Drive your elbows straight back past your body and squeeze your shoulder blades together at peak contraction.
- Let the handles return slowly, feeling a full stretch in the lats before initiating the next rep.

## Common mistakes

- Leaning too far back at the end of the pull, turning the row into a body-momentum exercise.
- Shrugging the shoulders up instead of keeping them depressed and retracted.
- Using too heavy a weight and pulling with the biceps instead of initiating with the back.

## FAQ

### Does narrow grip emphasize different muscles than wide grip?

Yes. Narrow grip targets the middle back (rhomboids, mid-traps) and lats, while wide grip shifts emphasis to the rear deltoids and upper traps. The narrow grip also allows a greater range of motion.

### Should I lean forward between reps?

A slight forward lean (10-15 degrees) at the start of each rep gives a better stretch on the lats. Avoid excessive leaning, which turns it into a lower back exercise.

### What muscles does the Narrow Grip Seated Row work?

The Narrow Grip Seated Row primarily targets the upper back, with secondary activation in the biceps, forearms. It's classified as a strength exercise for the back.

### Is the Narrow Grip Seated Row good for beginners?

Yes, the Narrow Grip Seated Row is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building upper back strength.
