# Standing Pelvic Tilt

## Quick answer

A small standing move where you tilt your pelvis to flatten your lower back. It helps the lower back move better and trains your abs to brace. Beginner back exercise using body weight. Targets lower back, also works abdominals.

- **Body Part:** Back
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Lower Back
- **Secondary Muscles:** Abdominals
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Mobility

---

## How to perform

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and your hands on your hips.
2. Tighten your stomach and tuck your hips under, flattening your lower back as if pressing it toward a wall behind you.
3. Hold for a few seconds, then relax back to the start.

## Pro tips

- Think about tucking your tailbone under and flattening your lower back against an imaginary wall behind you.
- Place one hand on your lower back and one on your belly to feel the muscles engage during the tilt.
- Move slowly and isolate the pelvis, keeping your upper body and knees as still as possible.

## Common mistakes

- Moving the entire upper body forward instead of isolating the Pelvic Tilt at the hips.
- Squeezing the glutes too aggressively, which limits the ability to feel the subtle pelvic movement.
- Holding breath during the exercise instead of maintaining relaxed, steady breathing.

## FAQ

### What is the purpose of a Standing Pelvic Tilt?

It teaches you to control your pelvic position, which is foundational for proper posture, core activation, and safe performance of exercises like squats and Deadlifts.

### How does this help lower back pain?

Many people have an excessive anterior Pelvic Tilt (lower back arched too much), which compresses the lumbar spine. Learning to posteriorly tilt the pelvis relieves this compression.

### How many should I do per day?

Perform 10-15 reps 2-3 times per day, especially if you sit for long periods. It takes only a minute and can be done anywhere.

### What muscles does the Standing Pelvic Tilt work?

The Standing Pelvic Tilt primarily targets the lower back, with secondary activation in the abdominals. It's classified as a mobility exercise for the back.

### Is the Standing Pelvic Tilt good for beginners?

Yes, the Standing Pelvic Tilt is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building lower back strength.
