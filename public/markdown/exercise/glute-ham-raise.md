# Glute Ham Raise

## Quick answer

A kneeling move on a glute ham raise machine where you lower your body forward and pull yourself back up with the backs of your legs. It works the hamstrings, with help from the glutes and lower back. Advanced legs exercise using body weight. Targets hamstrings, also works glutes, lower back.

- **Body Part:** Legs
- **Equipment:** Body Weight
- **Target Muscle:** Hamstrings
- **Secondary Muscles:** Glutes, Lower Back
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Adjust a glute ham raise machine to fit your body, then kneel on the pad with your ankles locked under the foot pads.
2. Cross your arms over your chest and kneel upright, body straight from knees to head.
3. Lower your upper body forward slowly, keeping your hips straight, until your body is nearly level with the floor.
4. Use your hamstrings and glutes to pull yourself back up to kneeling upright.

## Pro tips

- Lower yourself under control by eccentric hamstring contraction; do not just fall forward.
- Push your toes into the foot plate as you lift your body back up to engage both the hamstrings and glutes.
- Keep your hips extended throughout the movement; do not bend at the waist.

## Common mistakes

- Bending at the hips to initiate the upward movement, which turns it into a Back Extension instead of a hamstring exercise.
- Dropping too fast on the eccentric (lowering) phase, which wastes the most valuable part of the exercise.
- Not using the full range of motion by stopping short of a fully extended position at the bottom.

## FAQ

### Why is the Glute Ham Raise so difficult?

It requires the hamstrings to control your entire body weight through a long lever arm. It is one of the most demanding hamstring exercises because there is no mechanical advantage.

### What is a good alternative to the Glute Ham Raise?

Nordic Hamstring Curls are the closest alternative. Stability ball hamstring curls and slider curls are easier progressions that build toward the full Glute Ham Raise.

### How many Glute Ham Raises should I do?

Start with 3 sets of 5-8 reps if you are new to the exercise. Many people cannot do even one rep initially and should start with slow negatives (eccentric-only reps).

### What muscles does the Glute Ham Raise work?

The Glute Ham Raise primarily targets the hamstrings, with secondary activation in the glutes, lower back. It's classified as a strength exercise for the legs.

### Is the Glute Ham Raise good for beginners?

The Glute Ham Raise is an advanced exercise. Beginners should master fundamental legs movements first.
