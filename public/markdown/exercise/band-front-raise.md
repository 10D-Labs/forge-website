# Band Front Raise

## Quick answer

A front raise against a resistance band under your feet, lifting both straight arms to shoulder height. It works the front of the shoulders. Beginner shoulders exercise using resistance band. Targets shoulders, also works triceps, upper back.

- **Body Part:** Shoulders
- **Equipment:** Resistance Band
- **Target Muscle:** Shoulders
- **Secondary Muscles:** Triceps, Upper Back
- **Difficulty:** Beginner
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand on a resistance band with your feet shoulder-width apart. Hold an end in each hand in front of your thighs, palms facing down.
2. Keep your arms straight and raise them forward until they are level with the floor, at shoulder height.
3. Lower your arms slowly back to your thighs.

## Pro tips

- Step on the center of the band and hold the ends with palms facing down for a standard front raise.
- Raise your arms to shoulder height and hold for a one-second pause before lowering.
- Keep your core tight and avoid rocking your torso to swing the band up.

## Common mistakes

- Using momentum by swinging the torso to lift the band instead of strict shoulder flexion.
- Raising the arms above shoulder height, which recruits the upper traps and reduces front delt isolation.
- Stepping too far forward on the band, creating so much tension that form breaks down.

## FAQ

### Are Band Front Raises easier or harder than Dumbbell Front Raises?

Neither is universally easier. Bands are easier at the bottom and harder at the top. Dumbbells maintain more consistent difficulty throughout.

### Should you do front raises palms down or palms in?

Palms down (pronated) targets the anterior delt most. Palms in (neutral/thumbs up) reduces shoulder impingement risk and is better for people with shoulder sensitivity.

### How many sets of Band Front Raises should you do?

2-3 sets of 12-15 reps is sufficient. Front raises are an isolation accessory, not a primary movement.

### What muscles does the Band Front Raise work?

The Band Front Raise primarily targets the shoulders, with secondary activation in the triceps, upper back. It's classified as a strength exercise for the shoulders.

### Is the Band Front Raise good for beginners?

Yes, the Band Front Raise is rated as a beginner-level exercise. It's a great starting point for building shoulders strength.
