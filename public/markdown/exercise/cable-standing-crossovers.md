# Cable Standing Crossovers

## Quick answer

A standing cable move that brings your straight arms from out at your sides to crossed low in front of you. It works the chest, with help from the shoulders and triceps. Intermediate chest exercise using cable. Targets chest, also works shoulders, triceps.

- **Body Part:** Chest
- **Equipment:** Cable
- **Target Muscle:** Chest
- **Secondary Muscles:** Shoulders, Triceps
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Category:** Strength

---

## How to perform

1. Stand in the middle of a cable machine with your feet shoulder-width apart.
2. Hold a handle in each hand, palms facing down, with your arms straight out to the sides at shoulder height.
3. Keep your arms straight and bring your hands down and together in front of your body, crossing them over each other.
4. Return slowly until your arms are back out to the sides, keeping them straight.

## Pro tips

- Set the cables at the highest position and step forward into a slight lunge stance for stability.
- Bring your hands together in front of your lower chest and squeeze the pecs hard for a one-second hold.
- Keep a slight bend in the elbows throughout and control the return to the open-arm position.

## Common mistakes

- Bending the elbows excessively, which turns the fly into a pressing movement and reduces pec stretch.
- Leaning too far forward, which uses body weight to assist the movement instead of isolating the chest.
- Not controlling the eccentric, letting the cables pull the arms back too quickly.

## FAQ

### Are cable crossovers better than Dumbbell Flys?

Cable crossovers maintain constant tension throughout the full range of motion, especially at the peak contraction when the hands come together. Dumbbell Flys lose tension at the top.

### What is the best cable height for crossovers?

High cables target the lower chest. Low cables target the upper chest. Mid-height targets the mid-chest. Vary the height for complete chest development.

### Should crossovers be done before or after bench press?

After bench press as an isolation exercise, or before bench press as a pre-exhaustion technique. Most people benefit from doing them after compound pressing.

### What muscles does the Cable Standing Crossovers work?

The Cable Standing Crossovers primarily targets the chest, with secondary activation in the shoulders, triceps. It's classified as a strength exercise for the chest.

### Is the Cable Standing Crossovers good for beginners?

The Cable Standing Crossovers is rated as intermediate. Beginners should build a base of chest strength with easier variations first.
