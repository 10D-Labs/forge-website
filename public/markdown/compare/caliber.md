Forge puts an adaptive AI trainer with 24/7 chat in every plan for $19.99 a month. If you want a free tracker or a human watching your form on video, pick Caliber. ## Key Facts

- **Caliber:** Human coaching app with certified coaches at paid tiers. Free tier with the full tracker and a 500-plus exercise library, Pro group coaching at $19 a month, Premium 1:1 coaching from $200 a month. iOS and Android.
- **Forge:** AI personal trainer with four coaching personalities and a plan that adapts to your progress. $6.99 a week, $19.99 a month, $59.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial. iPhone and Android.
- **Core difference:** Caliber sells real human coaches and a full free tracker. Forge sells an AI trainer for $19.99 a month.

## The Short Version

Caliber built its reputation on human coaching. The free tier is the full app, ad-free, with a 500-plus exercise library and a tracker that has no business being free. Pay for Premium and a certified coach reviews your training weekly, checks your form on video, and answers daily chat. Programming follows a mastery-over-variety philosophy where you repeat the same core lifts for weeks and add weight, which is simply how strength gets built.

The price is the hard part. Premium starts at $200 a month, billed three months at a time, and you learn your exact rate only after a phone consultation. Lifters who train for novelty also struggle with the same lifts repeating for weeks, and the free tier never adjusts your program no matter what you log. Forge aims at that middle ground. For $19.99 a month the plan adapts continuously from your logged progress, with a trainer answering questions in character at any hour. That covers much of what people hire a coach for, at a tenth of Premium's price.

The honest comparison sits at the middle tier. Caliber Pro costs $19 a month for group coaching, within a dollar of Forge's monthly plan, so the choice there is a human coach in a group setting against an AI trainer who answers instantly at 2am. Caliber's 30-day refund and 4.9 App Store rating say the coaching delivers when you can afford it. If you want a free tracker, take Caliber's and never pay a cent. If you want coaching attention without a $200 bill, Forge fits.

## Where Caliber Wins

## FAQ

### Is Caliber's free version worth using?
Yes. It is the full tracker with the complete exercise library, ad-free, with no coach attached. Forge has nothing like it, since every Forge plan is paid after the 7-day trial. Self-directed lifters who write their own programs should take it.

### How much does Caliber coaching cost?
Pro group coaching runs $19 a month with a 7-day trial. Premium 1:1 coaching starts at $200 a month, billed three months at a time, and the exact figure comes out only in a phone consultation. A 30-day money-back guarantee covers paid tiers.

### Caliber Pro vs Forge: which $20 plan is better?
Caliber Pro gives you a human coach in a group setting with daily chat access. Forge gives you an adaptive plan and a trainer who answers instantly at any hour. Your pick hinges on whether a human name on your account matters more than an answer at midnight.

### Does Caliber have an Apple Watch app?
Caliber lists a dedicated watch app as coming soon, which we could not verify beyond that. Forge has no watch app either, so wrist logging is a wash between these two.

### Is Caliber good for cardio or CrossFit?
It is a strength app first. Cardio, CrossFit, and Olympic lifting sit outside its coaching model, and users looking for those report thin support. Forge also centers on lifting, so a conditioning-first athlete should look elsewhere.

### Can an AI trainer replace Caliber's human coach?
For programming and questions, mostly yes, and at a tenth of Premium's price. For watching your bar path on video and catching the form break you did not feel, no. That gap is the honest reason to pay Caliber's rate.
