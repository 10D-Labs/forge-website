## The Short Version

RP Hypertrophy App comes from Renaissance Periodization, the coaching company Dr. Mike Israetel built, and it programs the way he teaches. Training runs in mesocycles, with volume and loading adjusted each week from how you rate pump, soreness, and effort. Deloads arrive when the fatigue math says so. Over 250 technique videos from Dr. Mike sit inside the app, and lifters who follow the system describe the autoregulation as taking the guesswork out of hypertrophy.

The rigor asks a lot from the user. Every session you grade your own recovery, and beginners misjudge those signals often enough that the auto-volume can climb faster than they adapt. The interface feels dated at this price, the app stops working without internet, and a question about why your numbers changed has nowhere to go. Forge handles that last part differently. Your trainer explains each adjustment in the plan and answers around the clock, and the changes come from your logged progress, so a first-week user can follow along without learning to score their own soreness.

Cost is where most people walk away. RP runs $299.99 a year, skips any free tier or trial, and offers a 30-day refund window on direct purchases in place of a test drive. Its Trustpilot score sits at 2.8 largely on that complaint. Forge costs $59.99 a year with a 7-day free trial, less than two months of RP's standalone monthly plan. If you already speak RPE and want the deepest hypertrophy periodization in a consumer app, RP earns its price. Everyone else should start with Forge.

## FAQ

### Is RP Hypertrophy App worth the money?
For the right lifter, yes. If you understand RPE and RIR and want bodybuilding periodization without hiring a coach, the $299.99 buys programming depth nothing else in the app stores matches. If those acronyms are new to you, the same money buys a steep learning curve, and Forge at $59.99 a year is the easier place to start.

### Does RP Hypertrophy App have a free trial?
No. There is no free tier either, only a 30-day money-back guarantee on direct purchases, so testing the app means paying first. Forge gives you a 7-day free trial before any charge.

### Is RP Hypertrophy good for beginners?
The app assumes you can score your own pump, soreness, and effort accurately, and that self-rating is a skill most beginners have yet to develop. Misjudge it and the auto-volume can overshoot what you can recover from. Forge adjusts from logged performance instead, which asks much less of a new lifter.

### Does RP Hypertrophy App include nutrition coaching?
Not inside the training app. RP sells nutrition separately as RP Diet Coach. Forge also keeps its focus on training and offers no nutrition features, so neither subscription bundles diet help.

### Can you talk to a coach in RP Hypertrophy App?
There is no chat or coaching interaction. You get Dr. Mike's technique videos and the algorithm's weekly adjustments, and anything beyond that goes to a support inbox. Forge builds the product around exactly that conversation, with four trainer personalities answering in character around the clock.

### RP Hypertrophy vs Forge: which adapts better?
RP adapts deeper for hypertrophy. Its mesocycle logic and volume landmarks come from published bodybuilding methods, and Forge has no equivalent to that specialization. Forge adapts across your broader progress and feedback, and adds a coach you can talk to about every adjustment.
